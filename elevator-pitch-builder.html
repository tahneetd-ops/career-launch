STEP 1 OF 4
Who Are You?
Start with your name and one thing that defines who you are — not just "I'm a student." Think about a quality, a passion, or a role you play that's genuinely true.
Strong Examples:
"My name is Jordan, and I'm someone who loves building things from scratch — whether it's a model, a meal, or a plan."

"I'm Maya — I'm the kind of person who notices when someone needs help before they ask."

STEP 2 OF 4
What Are Your Strengths?
Name 1–2 genuine strengths — and back at least one up with a real example. "I'm a hard worker" means nothing without proof. "I once organized a whole school event by myself" means something.
Strong Examples:
"I'm great at staying calm when things go wrong — when our group project fell apart last week, I was the one who reorganized everyone."

"I have a real eye for design. I've been creating graphics for school events since 4th grade."

STEP 3 OF 4
Where Are You Headed?
Share your career direction or area of interest. It's okay to be exploring — but say it with intention. "I'm interested in" is fine. "I have no idea" is not a pitch.
Strong Examples:
"I'm really interested in working in health science — specifically anything that combines technology and helping people recover from injuries."

"I've been exploring the IT and cybersecurity cluster. I want to understand how to protect systems and prevent attacks."

STEP 4 OF 4
Your Closing Hook
End with something memorable — a question, a goal, or a statement that makes the person want to keep talking to you. This is what they'll remember.
Strong Examples:
"I'm here today because I want to find out what steps I should be taking right now — and I'd love to hear what you wish you'd known at my age."

"My goal is to be ready to start building toward that career before I even finish middle school."

✅ STRONG PITCH — 6th Grade Level
"My name is Darius, and I'm the kind of person who's always trying to figure out how things work — I take apart electronics just to see what's inside. My biggest strength is problem-solving under pressure. Last month I fixed our school's projector during a presentation when no one else knew what to do. I'm exploring the Information Technology cluster, especially cybersecurity, because I want to be someone who protects systems instead of breaking them. I'm here today because I want to know what I should be learning right now — and I'd love to hear what you wish you'd started earlier."
Why This Works:
✅ Opens with a specific personal description — not just a name
✅ Strength backed with a real story (fixed the projector)
✅ Career direction is specific — not just "technology"
✅ Closing hook is a genuine question — invites conversation
✅ Sounds like a real person talking, not a script being read
⭐ DECENT PITCH — Needs More Depth
"Hi, my name is Alexis. I'm a 6th grader who likes helping people. I'm good at talking to people and being nice. I want to be a nurse someday because I like science. Thank you."
What's Missing:
⚠️ "I like helping people" — too generic, everyone says this
⚠️ No real example of a strength in action
⚠️ "I like science" isn't a reason — what about nursing specifically?
⚠️ No closing hook — just "thank you"
✅ Has the right structure — just needs specifics filled in
❌ WEAK PITCH — Start Over
"Um... my name is Tyler. I don't really know what I want to do yet. I'm okay at some stuff. I like sports. I guess maybe something with sports or whatever. Yeah."
The Problems:
❌ "I don't really know" — you have to give them something
❌ "I'm okay at some stuff" — not a strength
❌ No direction, no hook, no reason to keep listening
❌ "Whatever" signals disengagement — that's the worst impression
💡 Tip: Even "I'm still figuring it out, but I've been exploring X" is better than this
